## INDIVIDUAL FIELD

### Can public entrants qualify for the Final?

Yes.

Public entrants and invited participants compete under the same sporting rules. Qualification is determined by performance, regardless of how the participant entered the event.

### How many athletes qualify for the Final?

Up to 48 athletes will progress to the Field Final.

The final qualification structure and any applicable category allocations will be confirmed in the official race rules.

### How does the Individual Field format work?

The Individual Field progresses through three competition stages:

1. Field Qualifying  
2. Grid TT  
3. Field Final

Field Qualifying is completed over eight laps. During laps 01–07, athletes complete four Trail laps and three Road laps in an open sequence. Lap 08 is completed on the Road loop.

The highest-placed qualifying athletes progress to Grid TT and the Field Final.

Before the Final, each qualified athlete completes one timed Road loop in Grid TT. Grid TT determines the starting order for the Final; its times do not carry into the Final result.

The Field Final is contested over 12 laps: six Trail laps and six Road laps. Athletes determine their own Road and Trail sequence before entering the final Road-only lap.

### How long are the Road and Trail loops?

The course consists of:

- Road Loop: approximately 660 metres
- Trail Loop: approximately 1,230 metres

Final course measurements may be adjusted following installation, testing and official course verification.

### How are Road and Trail laps recorded?

Each participant’s laps and times are recorded using an RFID timing system.

Course timing points and event officials will record which loop has been completed. Participants remain responsible for following the required Road and Trail sequence.

### What happens if I complete the wrong number of Road or Trail laps?

Participants must complete the prescribed number of Road and Trail laps.

Missing a lap, completing an additional lap or entering the wrong terrain may result in a time penalty, loss of position or disqualification. The final decision will be made by the race officials under the published race rules.


## COMMUNITY FIELD RELAY

### How many runners are required for a Relay team?

Each Community Field Relay team consists of six registered runners.

All six runners must complete their individual registration requirements before the team entry is confirmed.

### How does the Community Field Relay work?

The Community Field Relay is a separate six-person team competition with its own timing, ranking and podium.

Only one runner from each team may be active on the course at a time. Runners must complete their assigned Road or Trail leg before making an exchange inside the designated Relay Exchange Zone.

The Relay does not affect qualification, Grid TT positions or results in the Individual Field.

The final leg order and terrain allocation will be published in the official Relay rules.

### How is the Relay timed?

Each team is timed from the start of its first leg until the completion of its final leg.

The total time includes all running legs and exchanges. RFID timing and exchange-zone officials will be used to verify the result.

Penalties may be applied for an invalid exchange, missed timing point or incorrect course sequence.

### Does every Relay runner need to register separately?

Yes.

The team captain submits the team entry, but every runner must complete an individual participant form, provide emergency information and accept the event terms.

The team will not be confirmed until all six runners have completed registration.
